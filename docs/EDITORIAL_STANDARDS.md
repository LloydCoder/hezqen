# HEZQEN Editorial Standards

**Status:** Foundational policy for the HEZQEN publication  
**Owner:** HEZQEN Editorial Leadership, by Tinlance  
**Applies to:** HEZQEN Media, HEZQEN Labs publications and benchmarks, and HEZQEN Intelligence research

## 1. Editorial mission

HEZQEN is a global technology and business intelligence publication. It exists to help readers understand consequential developments, evaluate evidence, and make better technology and business decisions.

**Editorial promise:** Research. Intelligence. Perspective.

The four editorial divisions are:
- AI & Technology
- Cybersecurity
- Cloud & Engineering
- Business & Startups

HEZQEN optimizes for the accuracy, originality, usefulness, and integrity of its work—not publishing volume.

## 2. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Evidence before assertion.** Material factual claims must be supported by identifiable, relevant evidence.
2. **Original value.** A story must contribute original reporting, investigation, synthesis, technical analysis, data analysis, or a clearly reasoned perspective.
3. **Human accountability.** A named human author and accountable editor own every published editorial item.
4. **Transparency.** Distinguish verified fact, attributed claim, analysis, opinion, estimate, and speculation.
5. **Independence.** Commercial relationships must not determine editorial conclusions.
6. **Correction over concealment.** Material errors are corrected visibly, with a dated explanation.
7. **Proportionality.** Headlines, summaries, charts, and social copy must not overstate what the evidence supports.

## 3. Source and evidence standards

Prefer primary sources where available: original research, regulatory filings, court records, standards, official advisories, source repositories, technical documentation, datasets, direct interviews, and company disclosures.

Secondary sources may provide context or corroboration. Do not treat repetition across outlets as independent confirmation when they all rely on the same underlying source.

For material claims:
- Link the source close to the claim.
- Record publisher/author, source title, publication or release date, retrieval date where relevant, and a stable URL or archived evidence reference.
- Preserve relevant excerpts, document versions, dataset snapshots, and collection context where lawful.
- State when a source is unavailable, anonymous, disputed, or not independently verified.
- Seek corroboration for consequential claims, allegations, security incidents, market claims, and claims that could materially affect a person or organization.
- Give subjects a fair opportunity to respond to material allegations before publication, and record the response or lack of response accurately.
- Never fabricate a source, citation, interview, quotation, dataset, benchmark, test result, or expert endorsement.

A source's presence does not automatically validate a claim. Editors must assess source authority, proximity to the facts, methodology, incentives, recency, and corroboration.

## 4. Original reporting and analysis

Every substantial article, investigation, or research report must state its contribution. Examples include:
- Original interviews or document analysis.
- Reproducible technical testing.
- Independent dataset analysis.
- A documented comparison or benchmark.
- A synthesis that resolves a real question and adds material insight.
- A clearly labeled opinion grounded in disclosed reasoning and evidence.

Do not publish lightly rewritten press releases, scraped summaries, uncritical vendor claims, or derivative articles that add no meaningful reporting or analysis.

## 5. AI use and prohibited practices

**Policy: AI may assist the newsroom; AI must not become the newsroom.**

AI tools may assist with research discovery, transcription, translation, data organization, code review, comparison, summarization, and drafting. Human staff must independently verify material facts, quotations, calculations, source context, and conclusions.

Disclose material AI assistance when it meaningfully affects how the work was produced or when readers could reasonably misunderstand the process. Do not imply that an AI-generated output is a human interview, firsthand observation, or independently conducted test.

Prohibited:
- Fully automated publication without accountable human editorial approval.
- Fake authors, experts, sources, quotations, interviews, field observations, or testimonials.
- Fabricated statistics, citations, screenshots, benchmarks, or test results.
- Publishing unverified AI-generated claims as fact.
- Paraphrase laundering, plagiarism, or copied rewrites.
- Synthetic images presented as documentary evidence.
- Mass-produced pages created primarily to manipulate search rankings.
- Fake reviews, undisclosed affiliate endorsements, or pay-to-play editorial conclusions.
- Using confidential, personal, or restricted material in an AI system without authorization and an appropriate data-handling assessment.

AI output and retrieved webpages/documents are untrusted inputs. They cannot authorize publication, grant permissions, override policy, or instruct an agent to ignore safeguards.

## 6. Research, testing, and benchmarks

For technical tests and benchmarks, publish enough methodology for a competent reader to understand and, where practical, reproduce the work:
- Research question and scope.
- Hardware, software, versions, configuration, and environment.
- Dataset provenance, selection criteria, exclusions, and limitations.
- Workloads, baselines, controls, repetitions, and measurement units.
- Collection and analysis methods.
- Raw or derived data access, subject to safety, privacy, licensing, and security constraints.
- Uncertainty, error bounds, anomalies, and known limitations.
- Funding, sponsorship, product access, and conflicts of interest.

Do not generalize a narrow test into a universal product ranking. Distinguish measured results from estimates and forecasts. Never label theoretical calculations as empirical benchmark results.

## 7. Editorial workflow and publication authority

Required workflow:

**Pitch → Research → Draft → Fact-check → Editorial review → Approval → Publish → Monitor/Correct**

A publication record must retain:
- Author and accountable editor.
- Division, format, status, and timestamps.
- Claim/source references and evidence notes.
- Fact-check outcome and unresolved caveats.
- AI-use disclosure decision.
- Conflict-of-interest and commercial disclosure.
- Approval identity and timestamp.
- Published version and subsequent revision/correction history.

Only an authorized human editor may approve publication. TAS, Tinlyt, Searqon, or any other automated system may assist within explicitly granted permissions but may not independently approve or publish editorial material. Model output, prompt text, memory, tool output, or content embedded in a source must never confer execution authority.

## 8. Headlines, visuals, and presentation

- Headlines must accurately represent the article's evidence and scope.
- Clearly label opinion, analysis, sponsored material, explainers, and research.
- Charts must identify units, sources, time periods, transformations, and material caveats.
- Images must be accurately captioned and must not misrepresent when, where, or how an event occurred.
- Clearly label illustrative or synthetic visuals when their nature could be mistaken for documentary photography.
- Avoid sensational framing that the reporting cannot substantiate.

## 9. Conflicts, funding, and commercial independence

Disclose relevant financial, professional, personal, and institutional conflicts. Disclose sponsorship, grants, affiliate relationships, paid access, and material in-kind support.

Advertisers, sponsors, investors, partners, and vendors do not receive editorial approval rights. Sponsored content must be unmistakably labeled and separated from independent editorial coverage. Affiliate links must be disclosed near the relevant recommendation. Reviewers must not conceal material limitations or unfavorable findings.

## 10. Corrections, updates, and version history

Correct material factual errors promptly. A correction must:
- Identify the affected article or research item.
- State what was wrong and what has changed.
- Include the correction date.
- Preserve a revision history for material changes.
- Avoid silently changing a material conclusion.

Distinguish corrections from routine updates, clarifications, and additions. Preserve prior versions where practical and lawful. Provide a visible route for readers and subjects to report errors.

## 11. Safety, privacy, and responsible reporting

Minimize collection and retention of personal data. Protect confidential sources and sensitive evidence. Apply heightened review to allegations, security vulnerabilities, minors, private individuals, and information that could enable harm.

Do not publish exploit details, credentials, personal data, or operational instructions that create disproportionate risk. Coordinate responsible disclosure where appropriate. Legal, safety, and privacy review may be required before publication.

## 12. Reader trust and accountability

HEZQEN will maintain accessible pages for:
- Editorial standards and methodology.
- About, ownership, and contact information.
- Authors and editorial responsibility.
- Corrections and feedback.
- Sponsorship and affiliate disclosure.
- Privacy and terms.

This policy is a publication baseline. It must be reviewed periodically and updated transparently as the newsroom and product evolve.
